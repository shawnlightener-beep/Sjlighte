Passage 1: The Tryout

The Tryout

Marcus tightened the laces on his cleats as he stared at the baseball field. He had practiced for weeks, but now that tryouts had finally arrived, his confidence began to fade.

“Relax,” his older sister called from the bleachers. “You’ve prepared for this.”

Marcus stepped up to bat. The pitcher wound up and threw a fastball. Marcus swung too early. Strike one.

A murmur rippled through the small crowd. Marcus felt heat rise in his face. For a moment, doubt pressed against him heavier than his bat.

He inhaled slowly and repeated his coach’s advice in his mind: Focus on the ball, not the crowd.

The next pitch came faster than he expected. Marcus forced himself to stay steady. He watched the seams spin toward him and swung.

Crack!

The ball soared in a sharp arc across the outfield grass. Marcus sprinted toward first base, legs pumping, heart pounding—not with fear this time, but with resolve.

Later, as he walked off the field uncertain about the results, Marcus realized something unexpected. Whether he made the team or not, he had confronted his fear instead of retreating from it.

Passage 2: Why Bees Matter

Why Bees Matter

Bees may be small, but their impact on the planet is enormous. As pollinators, bees transfer pollen between flowers, enabling plants to reproduce. Without this process, many fruits and vegetables would not develop.

Scientists estimate that approximately one-third of the food consumed worldwide depends on pollination. Crops such as almonds, blueberries, cucumbers, and apples rely heavily on bees. In fact, some farmers transport beehives across states to ensure proper pollination.

However, bee populations have declined significantly over the past decade. Habitat destruction, pesticide exposure, disease, and climate change contribute to this decline. If bee populations continue to shrink, food prices could rise and ecosystems may become unstable.

Researchers are developing solutions, including planting pollinator-friendly gardens, reducing harmful chemicals, and supporting sustainable farming practices. Protecting bees ultimately protects global food security.
